Case study 01

RRR × FFF

I turned the select shop FFF in Itaewon, Seoul into a racing game — and brought the game back into the store for a two-week exhibition.

My role
Lead game designer & director
Team
4 people
Timeline
10-week build (Jun–Aug) + exhibition (Sep) 2026
Platform
Web-based mobile game (Unity 6) + 2 web interactions
Tools
Unity, Blender, Nomad Sculpt, Claude, three.js
Result
In-store exhibition at FFF, 5–19 Sep 2026
01 — Problem

A photo zone ends after one photo.

FFF already had a strong space and a loyal community. But the in-store experience stopped at a static photo spot, and the brand lost contact with visitors once they left Itaewon.

Problem 01

Static photo zones

One picture, then nothing. No reason to come back.

Problem 02

Online and offline apart

What happens in the store stays in the store.

Problem 03

Limited by location

Itaewon is a strong spot, but only for people who are there.

02 — Concept

Run, Rest, Refresh.

The project name is the design. I split time in the store into three states and gave each one its own content.

Run

Race through the store

Shelves, mannequins and racks become obstacles on the track. Players see the products while they run.

Rest

Stay and explore

Adventure mode removes the timer. Players walk the same map, pose their character and take photos.

Refresh

Leave with something new

Face tracking and a bubble installation turn visitors into the main character of the store.

03 — Level design

The real store became the track.

I did not invent a map. I blocked out the real FFF layout in grey boxes, ran through it in the engine, and split it into zones. The route decides which products players see, and in what order.

→
Start zoneOpening cinematic, a gentle stretch to learn the controls
Display zoneRacks, mannequins and real products as obstacles
Center pillarJump and dash combos, where the boost matters most
Final zoneThe most distorted space, finish line and ceremony
04 — Play

Understood in 30 seconds.

Nobody reads a tutorial in a shop. The controls are a virtual joystick, jump and dash. Everything is vertical 1080×1920, so every clip a player records is ready for Reels and TikTok.

05 — In-store interactions

Visitors became the main character.

Two browser-based installations ran in the store. They need only a webcam and a link, so many people can try them at once.

Don't Wake Up

A third-person mystery adventure set in the dream just before waking — where escaping reality and facing a new start pull in opposite directions.

01 — Why

Turning fear into a place.

Starting something new comes with excitement and fear. I put that feeling into an abstract space: the last moment of a dream before you wake up. The pressure you want to escape becomes a strange, beautiful shelter.

Feeling 01

Unfamiliar

You wake up outside a strange castle.

Feeling 02

Uneasy

The further you go, the more the world bends.

Feeling 03

Hopeful

The ending asks: "Do you want to wake up?"

02 — Level design

The further from reality, the stranger it gets.

A hub-and-rooms structure that loops back to the first door. Each room adds a new ability and more distortion.

OpeningCinematic arrival, lost and alone
Main HallHub — see the whole structure
Room 01Item + dash unlock
Room 02Falling Dream mini-game, new ability
Final RoomUse every item to escape
EndingBack to reality
RealityMost distorted
03 — Items & puzzles

Every item is a verb.

Each item gives a new way to move or see. Three puzzles ask the player to combine them, and the final room needs all of them.

🪽
WingsDouble jump · flight

Reach higher platforms and fly from jump pads.

👢
BootsDash

Cross gaps and move fast through the hub.

👓
GlassesSee the unseen

Toggle on to reveal hidden objects and find the real wings.

🗡️
SwordBreak obstacles

Unlocked by waking up in Falling Dream, my solo mini-game.

Falling Dream

A dream of endless falling before you wake. A short survival mini-game I designed, built and wired into Don't Wake Up on my own.

My role
Everything: design, art direction, Unity build, integration
Team
Solo
Timeline
Mar–Apr 2026

Genre
Casual survival mini-game · 2.5D falling

01 — Concept

Falling, but not scary.

A 3D character falls through a 2D space and dodges obstacles. Light, cute motions keep the tension low, so anyone who likes quick casual games can pick it up.

Play

2.5D falling

A 3D character in a flat, endless fall. Obstacles come from every side.

Controls

Four keys only

Up, down, left, right. No tutorial needed beyond one on-screen hint.

Mood

Cold and hushed

A cold, quiet dream space with heavy, sunken air, softened by playful character motion.

02 — System

You play to wake up.

The mini-game is not a side activity. It is a story device that moves the player from reality into a dream and back, and it changes what they can do in the main game.

Core system

Wake Gauge

Keep dodging while you fall and the gauge rises. At 100 you wake up and the game is cleared.

Asleep100 = Awake
Link to the main game

Item unlock

Clearing the dream unlocks the sword in Don't Wake Up. The sword breaks obstacles there, so this short game opens the path forward in the main story.

03 — Play flow

From reality into the dream, and back.

01Find a sword stuck in the bed frame
02It's locked — you can't take it yet
03Touch the bed and fall into the dream
04Dodge obstacles while falling
05Wake Gauge hits 100 — clear
06The sword is unlocked
04 — Ending

A slow landing.

I designed the clear as a short cinematic, so the reward feels like waking from a real dream.

  1. The fall slows down
  2. The player lands on the bed
  3. The sword appears in the headboard
  4. The player reaches out and grabs it
  5. Fade to black
  6. Back to reality, sword in hand
